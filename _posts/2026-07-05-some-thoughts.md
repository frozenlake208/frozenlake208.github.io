Well, half of 2026 is already gone. Today is July 5th, 2026, and it feels like a good time to sit down and reflect on how these months have gone.

I actually started this blog back at the beginning of April 2026, but the content was not really mine — the AI did about 100% of the work, and I just felt ashamed of it. Every line and every bit of knowledge felt like it did not belong to me. So for that simple reason, I am starting over. This time I will do it myself and try not to over-rely on AI.

Looking back at 2025, that was a rough year. The part it hit hardest was my mental health, then my inner peace, my career path, and so on. I do not want to dwell on it too much, but one positive thing is that it made me a little stronger — even if I do not really feel it yet.

I promised myself I would come back more determined in 2026, but now that half the year is gone, I do not feel like I have achieved anything remarkable. After a lot of technical and behavioral interviews, I finally landed a permanent job with an average salary — enough that I no longer depend on my parents' allowance. And luckily, the environment suits me well, and I got along with my colleagues quickly.

I planned to retake my IELTS test this month, but it no longer looks feasible, so I have to postpone it. I hope I can get it done before my 23rd birthday.

I think all of my negative feelings come from the same root: a lack of self-discipline, constant procrastination, and illusory effort. I plan a lot and imagine myself achieving things one day, but all of it just sits in my head — I never take real action. "Take action first, reward later." I know the principle, yet I never actually follow it.

I spend my time scrolling short videos on social media instead of focusing on work that actually matters. Because of that, I never really gain deep knowledge — everything I "learn" is just the tip of the iceberg.

I have also noticed that I am far too dependent on AI. To solve a task, I barely think for a moment before writing a prompt and waiting for the result. The real problem is that I do not care much about the output — I just copy, paste, and run it without any verification. I know that if this keeps going, my logical thinking will be seriously affected. This is something I really need to watch out for.

That is probably enough words. These are just some thoughts I wanted to write down for myself. Time does not wait for anyone, and if I keep living the way I did in these first six months, I know I will end 2026 full of regret, blaming myself.

So from now on, my words are: take action, get out of my comfort zone, and stop comparing myself to others. The only person I need to beat is yesterday's me.
